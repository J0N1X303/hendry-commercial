About

I know what it feels like to sit on both sides of the negotiation.

My commercial foundations were forged in hospitality, from prestigious events to Walt Disney World, where standards are visible, service is immediate, and small details have a disproportionate impact on the experience. It taught me early that commercial performance and customer experience are rarely separate problems.

From there, I moved to the buying side, managing significant spend and commercial decisions in foodservice before crossing the table into sales, client leadership, and ultimately company-wide commercial responsibility.

Most recently, as Head of Sales & Client Director at Expression for Growth, I was accountable across a £3m revenue base, leading sales performance, forecasting, pricing, margin, enterprise growth, and commercial planning.

That mix matters. I don’t look at growth purely through a sales lens. I think about what the customer will actually buy, where value is being created, what makes a proposition credible, where margin disappears, and what has to happen operationally to deliver it.

Curiosity is the thread that connects all of it. I tend to question the default, pull apart how something works, and look for a simpler or more commercially useful route. AI is now part of how I work every day: researching, analysing, prototyping, automating, and testing ideas faster. I’m less interested in AI as a label than in what it lets you remove, improve, or do that wasn’t practical before.

When I found myself navigating redundancy, I did what I tend to do with messy problems: broke it down, built a system, tested it, and looked for a way to make it useful beyond me.
