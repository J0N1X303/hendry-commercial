The invisible buying journey

The customer may already be deciding before you know they exist.

Buyers can use search, AI assistants, reviews, comparison tools, public records, colleagues and your own website to investigate you long before anyone becomes a lead. The interface changes. The buying questions do not.

The argument

The objective is not to become “good at AI.” It is to make it easier for the right customer to make a confident buying decision.

AI matters because it changes the economics of research. Asking another question is cheap. Comparing three providers takes seconds. Buyers can add constraints, test claims and narrow a shortlist without speaking to anyone.

That makes public commercial truth more important: what you do, who you are for, what can be proved, why you are different, what the trade-offs are and what happens next.

The book turns that idea into a practical operating system rather than another set of tactics for chasing an algorithm.

Why I wrote it

I wanted something durable enough to outlive the current AI acronyms.

There is already plenty being written about AEO, GEO, AI SEO and whatever label comes next. Some of it is useful. But the commercial question underneath is older and more important: can a customer find enough accurate information, understand the proposition, verify what matters, justify a shortlist and take the next step?

My career has crossed both sides of that table — procurement, enterprise sales, P&L ownership, pricing, forecasting, commercial strategy and building new things. Buyer Readiness is an attempt to turn that perspective into something practical.
